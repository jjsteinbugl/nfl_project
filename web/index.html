      How this is measured

      Openness is the distance from a receiver to the nearest coverage defender when the ball would arrive. It starts from everyone's position, speed and direction at the throw and projects them forward for the ball's estimated air time (about 0.08 s + 0.05 s per yard, fitted on this data), capped at 1 second since defenders react after that. Checked against tracked positions at arrival, this projection matches the real separation closely (correlation 0.86).

      Decisions: a target within yd of the most open receiver counts as "most open". A target with at least yd is "open". When the target wasn't open, an open alternative (≥ yd, and ≥ yd more open) at a similar or greater depth is a missed open receiver; if the only open alternatives were at least yd shallower, the QB chose depth over a checkdown.

      Pass blockers: the data lists every eligible player as a route runner, including RBs, TEs and fullbacks who stayed in to block. Standing in the pocket far from coverage defenders, they would look wide open. A back or tight end who never got more than 1 yd past the line and, at the throw, is still behind it, within 5 yd of the QB and nearly stopped (under 2.5 yd/s) is treated as a pass blocker and left out of the comparison. They're still listed, marked "Pass blocking", in the play table.

      Limits: this sees only route runners and coverage defenders (no pass rush), so it can't tell when pressure forced the throw, a receiver hadn't turned around yet, or the play call dictated the read. Treat it as a guide to interesting plays, not a final grade.
